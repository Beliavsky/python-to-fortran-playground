Title: --realloc-lhs-arrays gives intermittent wrong results for v = v + 1

Environment tested:
Windows, LFortran 0.66.0, LLVM 21.1.7,
default target x86_64-pc-windows-msvc, initialized VS 2022 BuildTools.
Linux and newer LFortran versions have not been tested.

Reproducer: lfortran_realloc_lhs.f90 in this directory.
No external libraries or helper modules are needed.

Commands (run from an initialized MSVC command prompt):
  lfortran --version
  lfortran --realloc-lhs-arrays lfortran_realloc_lhs.f90 -o repro.exe
  repro.exe
  repro.exe
  repro.exe

Expected output (ignoring whitespace):
  10 20 30
  3
  40 50
  2
  41 51

Observed on October 8, 2026: compilation and execution exit successfully.
Initial allocation and resizing are correct, but the final self-assignment
sometimes fails. Three consecutive executions of the same binary printed:
  1 1
  41 51
  7471105 6029434
for the final vector. Other runs have printed 1 51 or other incorrect values.

The same failure occurs with our full playground flags:
  lfortran --no-style-suggestions --no-color --implicit-interface --separate-compilation --legacy-array-sections --realloc-lhs-arrays lfortran_realloc_lhs.f90 -o repro.exe

GNU control (verified correct):
  gfortran -std=f2018 -fcheck=all lfortran_realloc_lhs.f90 -o gnu.exe
  gnu.exe

The varying results suggest an invalid memory access or prematurely released
RHS storage, but that is an inference, not a diagnosed compiler cause.

Issue tracker: https://github.com/lfortran/lfortran/issues
A search for realloc/self-assignment and realloc/wrong-result reports found
related issues, but no exact match was established. In particular #12970
concerns derived-type self-assignment, not this plain integer vector case.
Before filing, check for a duplicate and attach the source and version output.
